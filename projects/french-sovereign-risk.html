Research brief / 01 / Macro & credit

French sovereign stress and European bank risk

A research programme examining the transmission of French sovereign borrowing pressures into European bank funding, capital and security prices.

Research statusIn development
Subject areaMacro & credit
PublicationFull findings not yet published

Research question

Question under investigation

Are selected European bank securities appropriately priced for a scenario of sustained French sovereign-bond stress?

Why this matters

Banks can be exposed to sovereign stress through direct securities holdings, mark-to-market losses, collateral values, funding markets and the real economy. These channels differ by bank and by regulatory treatment.

A wider French–German government bond yield spread does not automatically imply a profitable short in bank equities. The research must isolate exposures and assess how much risk is already reflected in market prices.

Planned approach

  • Establish the chronology of French fiscal developments, OAT–Bund yield spreads and relevant central-bank conditions.
  • Use banks’ published reports to compare sovereign exposures, capital headroom, liquidity and funding composition.
  • Examine selected bank equity or bond prices alongside sector and sovereign benchmarks; distinguish changes in credit risk from general market moves.
  • Develop base, stress and severe-stress cases; quantify plausible balance-sheet or valuation effects where the evidence supports doing so.
  • Select one traded security and formulate a long, short or no-trade conclusion, including explicit thesis invalidation criteria.

Evidence and sources

Sources to be assessed as the project develops include:

  • ECB and Banque de France releases; Eurostat and government debt data
  • Issuer annual reports and Pillar 3 disclosures
  • Bond yields, bank securities and relevant benchmark series from appropriately licensed public datasets
  • Contemporaneous policy statements and market disclosures

Critical limitations

Separate correlation from causation. Accounting treatment, supervisory capital treatment, market collateral values and lending behaviour are related but not interchangeable. Avoid inferring a uniform impact across European banks.

Publication note. This page is a research brief, not a completed report. No return forecasts, investment recommendations, substantive empirical findings or completed analyses are claimed. Final conclusions will be added only after the underlying work has been finished and checked.
