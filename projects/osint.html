Research brief / 03 / Open-source intelligence

OSINT, due diligence and risk briefings

A developing series of short, source-traceable investigations using public records to assess corporate, security and geopolitical risks.

Research statusOngoing practice
Subject areaOpen-source intelligence
PublicationFull findings not yet published

Research question

Question under investigation

What can independently verified public information establish about a company, network, security incident or geopolitical development — and what remains unknown?

Why this matters

Good OSINT is not a collection of search results. It is a documented analytical process that separates source claims, independently verified facts and judgements.

For corporate and intelligence audiences, provenance, proportionality and information handling are as important as a polished final brief.

Planned approach

  • Define a specific decision-oriented research question and a proportionate scope.
  • Gather data from lawful public sources, prioritising primary records, official statements, corporate filings and independently corroborated reporting.
  • Maintain a source log with dates, URLs, claims, evidence quality and outstanding contradictions.
  • Cross-check entities, timelines and geographies; record confidence levels and alternative explanations.
  • Publish a concise briefing with key judgements, evidence gaps and recommendations for further collection.

Evidence and sources

Sources to be assessed as the project develops include:

  • Company registries, official filings and court materials where lawful and appropriate
  • Government and multilateral reports; sanctions and procurement data
  • Archive links, public geospatial data and reputable reporting
  • Publicly accessible, non-sensitive sources only

Critical limitations

Public-source visibility is incomplete. Do not publish private contact details, sensitive personal data, confidential client information or research involving intrusive identification of private individuals.

Publication note. This page is a research brief, not a completed report. No return forecasts, investment recommendations, substantive empirical findings or completed analyses are claimed. Final conclusions will be added only after the underlying work has been finished and checked.
