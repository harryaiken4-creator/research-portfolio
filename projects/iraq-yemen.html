Research brief / 05 / Political risk & conflict data

Iran-aligned armed groups: Iraq and Yemen, 2020–2024

A final-year university dissertation studying whether regional shocks or theatre-specific conflict dynamics are associated with changes in armed-group activity.

Research statusDissertation in progress
Subject areaPolitical risk & conflict data
PublicationFull findings not yet published

Research question

Question under investigation

To what extent is the use of force by Iran-aligned armed groups in Iraq and Yemen associated with shared regional shocks rather than theatre-specific conflict dynamics?

Why this matters

Iran-aligned groups may share regional priorities without being centrally directed in every operation. Descriptive co-movement alone cannot establish coordination.

Comparing theatres demands careful event definitions, especially when reported activity includes unverified claims, interceptions or events coded outside the group’s immediate theatre.

Planned approach

  • Define the actor universe, event inclusion rules and comparable time periods for Iraq and Yemen.
  • Code source notes consistently, distinguishing claims of attacks from reported attempts and confirmed events.
  • Create aligned weekly event series with documented choices for country, actor and event type.
  • Evaluate activity around major regional shocks while considering theatre-specific changes and reporting biases.
  • Use descriptive comparisons and appropriate statistical models without asserting causal direction from simple correlations.

Evidence and sources

Sources to be assessed as the project develops include:

  • ACLED event data subject to its licensing and citation requirements
  • Relevant primary actor statements, where ethically appropriate
  • Credible independent conflict reporting and regional event chronologies
  • Academic research on Iranian-aligned armed groups and regional networks

Critical limitations

Events databases and actor claims are not equivalent to confirmed military actions. Inference about organisational control or operational coordination must be limited by observability and source quality.

Publication note. This page is a research brief, not a completed report. No return forecasts, investment recommendations, substantive empirical findings or completed analyses are claimed. Final conclusions will be added only after the underlying work has been finished and checked.
