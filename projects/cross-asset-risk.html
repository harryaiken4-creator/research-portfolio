Research brief / 04 / Quantitative markets

Can risk indicators anticipate equity drawdowns?

A reproducible R study evaluating whether widely followed trend, credit and breadth indicators add useful information about subsequent equity-market risk.

Research statusPlanned
Subject areaQuantitative markets
PublicationFull findings not yet published

Research question

Question under investigation

Do credit conditions, trend measures and market breadth improve forecasts of future equity drawdowns beyond simple historical benchmarks?

Why this matters

Indicators that appear effective in backtests can be explained by overfitting, overlapping signals and decisions made with information unavailable at the time.

The aim is to assess predictive usefulness rather than assemble a dashboard of attractive-looking technical indicators.

Planned approach

  • Define a target outcome such as next-month drawdown, future volatility or a binary risk-off event.
  • Construct features from actual corporate bond yield spreads, market breadth, volatility and trend, clearly defining publication lags.
  • Compare simple benchmark models with indicator-based approaches using walk-forward or held-out tests.
  • Measure hit rates, false positives, calibration and sensitivity to transaction costs where a trading interpretation is appropriate.
  • Present useful and unsuccessful results equally; publish reproducible R scripts and a data dictionary.

Evidence and sources

Sources to be assessed as the project develops include:

  • FRED or equivalent documented public yield spread series
  • Historical benchmark equity index and volatility data
  • Clearly attributed market-breadth data with available history
  • Published research on forecasting, leakage and model validation

Critical limitations

ETF price ratios such as HYG/SHY or HYG/LQD are proxies, not literal corporate credit-spread series. Backtests must avoid look-ahead bias, survivorship bias and repeated tuning to the same sample.

Publication note. This page is a research brief, not a completed report. No return forecasts, investment recommendations, substantive empirical findings or completed analyses are claimed. Final conclusions will be added only after the underlying work has been finished and checked.
